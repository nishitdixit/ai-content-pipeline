# 03: Voice & Style Guide

This guide is for me when I record and for the AI when it writes scripts. The script engine's prompt will be built from this document.

## The voice in one line
**A calm older brother or friend who has been through the rush, speaking to you at 10 PM on the terrace.**

## Personality
| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Calm, unhurried | Loud, hyped |
| Warm, kind | Preachy, superior |
| Honest ("main bhi aisa karta tha") | A guru with all the answers |
| Practical | Abstract or philosophical for its own sake |
| Gently funny sometimes | Sarcastic or mocking |
| Respectful of tradition | Religious or ritualistic |

## How the Hinglish should sound
I speak the way I normally talk: mostly Hinglish, slipping into Hindi or English when it feels natural.

**Rules**
1. **Write the way I'd speak, not the way I'd write.** Short sentences. Pauses. "Na?", "Matlab...", "Socho zara."
2. **Use English for modern ideas:** career, deadline, LinkedIn, burnout, salary, notification.
3. **Use Hindi for feelings and wisdom:** dil, sukoon, thahrav, santosh, bhaag-daud, apne aap se.
4. **Sanskrit only in short quotes**, always followed immediately by a simple meaning.
5. **Use Roman script** (Latin letters) in scripts and subtitles. It's easier to read from a teleprompter and wider-reaching. Use Devanagari only for decorative words on screen.
6. **Use "tum"** to sound like a friend. Avoid "aap" (too formal) and "tu" (too casual for strangers).
7. **Use "main" and "hum" a lot.** I'm in this with them.

**Example: good vs. bad**

❌ *"Aaj hum discuss karenge ki how to stop comparing yourself with others using 5 powerful strategies."*

✅ *"Kabhi raat ko LinkedIn kholke laga hai... ki sab aage nikal gaye? Main bhi ye karta tha. Daily."*

## Hooks: formulas that fit our voice
| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| A question that hurts a little | "Tumhe bhi lagta hai tum late ho gaye?" |
| A myth to break | "25 tak settle hona zaroori nahi hai." |
| "Main bhi" confession | "Main ek saal tak sirf bhaagta raha. Kahan pahuncha? Kahin nahi." |
| A scene the viewer recognises | "Raat ke 1 baje, phone haath mein, neend gayab..." |
| An ancient line, then a modern twist | "5000 saal pehle Krishna ne ek baat boli thi jo tumhare Monday ke liye hai." |
| Permission | "Aaj kuch nahi karna — ye bhi theek hai." |

**Hook rules:** under 10 words where possible. No "Hello dosto, aaj ki video mein..." The hook comes first and the greeting never does.

## Words and phrases
**Signature phrases** (use them sometimes, not in every video, so they stay special):
- "Ruko zara."
- "Aaram se."
- "Zindagi race nahi hai."
- "Tum late nahi ho."
- "Thoda thehro."

**Words to avoid:** hustle (except when reframing it), grind, alpha, crush it, "boss babe", "100% guaranteed", "life badal jaayegi", "secret".

## Handling scripture and quotes
- **Always cite the source:** "Bhagavad Gita, adhyay 2, shlok 47." If it's not verified, don't use it.
- **Check every quote** against a reliable translation before recording. The script engine must flag quotes for verification; it must never present them as fact without checking.
- **Explain, don't preach.** "Krishna kehte hain..." → "Matlab simple hai..."
- Never claim one religion is better than another. Kabir, Buddha, Sufi poets and Stoics are all welcome alongside the Gita.

## Sensitive topics
- **Mental health:** I share perspective, not treatment. If a script touches on anxiety or depression, end with: *"Agar ye feeling bahut heavy hai, kisi se baat karo — dost, family, ya professional. Tele-MANAS helpline 14416 pe free baat kar sakte ho."*
- **Money and career:** never tell people to quit their jobs. The message is "rush mat karo", not "kuch mat karo".
- **Relationships, marriage, family pressure:** be gentle and avoid blaming parents or society. Acknowledge both sides.

## On-camera delivery
- **Speak slower than feels natural.** Calm is the brand.
- **Pause after the hook and before the takeaway.** Silence is fine.
- **Look into the lens**, not at the screen.
- **Smile with your eyes** at the soft close.
- **Don't read word for word.** Read the script once, then say it in your own words. Being authentic matters more than being perfect.
